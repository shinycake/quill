Quill is an unofficial Telegram client and still in beta.

### Hebrew and Arabic on Windows

- Hebrew and Arabic are no longer drawn backwards on Windows (#1490 by `@tomerbasat10-debug`). Chat names, previews, avatar initials and message text now read in the right order, and a trailing space stays on the correct side of a right-to-left line.
- Names next to punctuation, Hebrew points (niqqud) and tabs inside right-to-left text are also placed correctly on Windows (#1491).

### Right-to-left text everywhere

- A wrapped Hebrew line now reads top to bottom in service messages, link previews and code blocks (#1491).
- A link or mention in the middle of a Hebrew sentence keeps its own color, underline and highlight (#1491).
- Hebrew names in sentences no longer pull the colon, "and" or quote marks to the wrong side. This covers service messages, the typing line, the chat list, pinned messages, the window title, the archive row and the group call chat (#1491, #1493).
- "Forwarded from" lines, Hebrew bulleted and numbered lists, and captions that mix Hebrew and English each follow their own reading direction (#1493).
- A Hebrew chat-list preview with custom emoji reads in the right order (#1491).
- Search finds "שלום" in "שָׁלוֹם": highlights ignore Hebrew points and Arabic vowel marks (#1493).
- Search, rename and other one-line fields lay Hebrew out right to left (#1491).

### Typing Hebrew and Arabic

- Double-click selects a whole Hebrew or Arabic word (#1491).
- The caret never stops inside an emoji or between a letter and its points, and Backspace after a point removes only that point (#1491, #1493).
- The arrow keys move across the rows of a wrapped Hebrew paragraph, and they move the right way right after a draft is restored (#1491).

### Under the hood

- Text layout is now checked against one shared set of rules on the real text engine of each system (CoreText on macOS, DirectWrite on Windows, cosmic-text on Linux) in CI (#1491).

### Not yet tested on a live account

- These changes were checked with tests, CI on all three systems and offline demos on macOS. They were not run on real Windows or Linux hardware.
