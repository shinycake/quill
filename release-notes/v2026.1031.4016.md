### Quill stays light through a long session

The last release fixed the big leak in animated stickers. This one goes after what built up slowly over hours of use: message text, pictures in other windows, stickers and emoji nobody was looking at any more, and windows that kept drawing when nothing on them changed.

- Message text takes far less memory. Each laid-out line carried room for 32 styles where it almost always uses one, and Quill kept each message laid out at up to four widths, two of which nothing ever draws. It now keeps only the widths a message can be drawn at, within 64 MB in all, where a long scroll through a channel of long posts used to hold well over 100 MB. Scrolling back stays as smooth as before.
- Animated stickers and emoji close 5 seconds after the last window stops showing them, with their frames. Before, closing the emoji panel over a chat of plain text kept every clip from it open, up to 96 MB, until a sticker played again.
- A chat opened in its own window keeps its pictures within a budget, like the main window. Before, every picture it showed stayed in memory until Quill quit.
- Avatars and custom emoji go into small GPU textures of their own, so one left on screen no longer keeps a 4 MB texture alive.
- Ads, poll results and profile details of chats Quill has let go of are released with them.
- A closed video gives back what macOS kept for it. 200 videos opened and closed used to leave 995 video clocks behind.
- On a Mac, a window that keeps a small animation going hands back its spare screen buffer, about 20 MB, as an idle window already did. And more windows now go idle: a paused voice note or song redrew 4 times a second, a message effect you hadn't seen yet redrew 120 times a second behind other apps, and a field focused in a window in the background blinked its hidden caret.

### Less CPU, and a Mac that can sleep

- Quill closes the speakers half a second after the last sound. Before, one notification sound kept the speakers open, and macOS awake, for as long as Quill ran. Muted clips no longer hold the speakers either.
- Opening the speakers takes up to 75 ms on a Mac. Quill now does it on a thread of its own instead of the one that draws the window, so the first sound, and every voice note, video or call sound after a quiet spell, starts without stalling the window.
- On a 120 Hz Mac, a clip and animated emoji in the same chat each drew on their own schedule, so the window drew about twice as often as either needed. They now share one 60 Hz tick: 52 frames a second down to 33, and 12.7% CPU down to 9.6%, for a clip beside three emoji.
- Once any app had read the window's layout (window managers like Tiles do it on a click), Quill redrew the whole window on every frame until it quit, about 18% CPU with nothing happening. It now redraws only what changed, as before.
- With the history scrolled up, a clip passing the bottom of the chat redrew the whole chat 30 times a second. CPU 16% down to 11.5%.

### Also fixed

- Clicking the jump-to-latest button, the date pill or the corner buttons over a video or photo also opened the media viewer on it.
