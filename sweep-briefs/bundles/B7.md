## Full findings

## media-7: A Linux viewer picture is copied three times and, above 1024 px, gets a GPU texture of its own
- Severity: P2 measurable waste
- Kind: cpu
- Platforms: Linux. Windows uses NV12 surfaces and macOS AVPlayer surfaces. Inline tiles stay under 1024 px and share atlas textures, so they pay the copies only.
- Where: `src/video_decode/ffi.rs:533` → `rotate_bgra` (`mod.rs:270`): a fresh `Vec` per picture, even at rotation 0. Then `third_party/gpui-pre-wgpu/src/wgpu_atlas.rs:308-317` and `:435-446`: `bytes.to_vec()` per upload. Then `queue.write_texture`'s staging copy. `wgpu_atlas.rs:223-306`: a picture wider than 1024 px takes `push_texture` and `create_texture` every time, and `remove` frees it after retirement (`:162-181`).
- Trigger: any 1080p clip in the viewer or a story on Linux.
- Effect and size: inferred. 8.3 MB × 30/s = 250 MB/s per copy, so about 750 MB/s of memcpy on top of swscale, plus 30 texture creates and destroys a second, and a new 8 MB `Vec` each time (mmap and page faults on glibc). The swscale share is quality-sweep #18, still open. This adds the proof for it: `SWS_ACCURATE_RND` (`quillvideo.c:486,555`) turns off the fast YUV to RGB path, `!(flags & SWS_ACCURATE_RND)` at `libswscale/swscale_unscaled.c:2428` in the n9 source, and tdesktop passes flags 0 (`ffmpeg_utility.cpp:574-583`).
- Why it is real: `video-player-nv12.md:40` left Linux on BGRA because the wgpu renderer was a registry crate that draws no surfaces. It is vendored now (`third_party/gpui-pre-wgpu`), so that reason has gone.
- Fix sketch: in steps. Drop `SWS_ACCURATE_RND`. Hand the shim a Rust-owned, pooled buffer so `turn_plane_into` copies nothing at rotation 0. Keep one reusable texture per player above 1024 px. The real fix is NV12 surfaces in the vendored wgpu renderer, sharing Windows' path.
- Proof plan: `convert_us` in the shim (already reported) and a `--video-probe` timing over a 1080p fixture in the Linux CI job. A debug counter for textures created while a clip plays: expect flat after the fix.
- Scope: `native/quillvideo/quillvideo.c`, `src/video_decode/{ffi,mod,nv12}.rs`, `third_party/gpui-pre-wgpu`. Medium to high risk for the surface step. Overlaps the Linux/Windows lane.

## linux-windows-6: Linux video pictures still pay BGRA conversion, two UI-thread copies and a new texture per frame
- Severity: P2 measurable waste
- Kind: cpu
- Platforms: Linux. macOS and Windows already draw NV12 surfaces.
- Where: `src/ui/video_player/surface.rs:23` `NV12_SURFACES = cfg!(target_os = "windows")` (macOS has its own path), `gpui-pre-wgpu/src/wgpu_renderer.rs:1744-1746` where `PrimitiveBatch::Surfaces(_) => {}` does nothing, and `wgpu_atlas.rs` `upload_texture` and `swizzle_upload_data` (`bytes.to_vec()`, the first copy), `flush_uploads` (`queue.write_texture`, the second copy), `push_texture` (a new texture per picture wider than 1024).
- Trigger: playing any video on Linux.
- Effect and size: measured in `docs/decisions/video-player-nv12.md:192-195`: 1080p BGRA is 8,294,400 bytes and 5.23 ms to convert, while NV12 is 3,110,400 bytes and 0.78 ms. At 720p it is 2.25 ms against 0.27 ms. The two copies add about 2 to 4 ms per 1080p frame on the UI thread (guess, from 5 to 10 GB/s memcpy). At 60 fps that is a large share of the frame budget, and `src/video_decode` sets up no hardware decoder to offset it. The viewer caps at 1920 wide (`OpenOptions::viewer`), so 4K is not worse.
- Why it is real: the doc's reason for skipping Linux at lines 40-41 is that wgpu was a registry crate and not vendored. `Cargo.toml:359` now vendors `gpui-pre-wgpu`, so that reason is gone.
- Fix sketch: port `gpui-pre-windows`' `yuv_surfaces.rs` to WGSL (R8 plus RG8 planes, slot reuse, `mark_drawn` and refused handling) and turn `NV12_SURFACES` on for Linux. As an interim step, drop the `to_vec` when no swizzle is needed.
- Proof plan: `QUILL_TRACE_VIDEO` lines under Xvfb and lavapipe with the 1080p fixture, comparing convert time and frame pacing, plus a screenshot to catch a color matrix error.
- Scope: `gpui-pre-wgpu` (renderer, shaders, atlas), `surface.rs`. Medium risk. Overlaps the media lane.
