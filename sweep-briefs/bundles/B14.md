## robustness-8: Combining-mark and invisible-character abuse is not filtered from message text
- Severity: P3
- Kind: robustness
- Platforms: macOS, Windows, Linux (shaping backends differ; the Linux stack is the most exposed)
- Where: `src/ui/message_text/paragraph.rs` passes text to shaping with no filter. A grep across `src` finds no equivalent of tdesktop's limits.
- Trigger: someone sends a message with hundreds of combining marks per letter, or Unicode tag characters. A hostile message needs no action from the reader beyond opening the chat.
- Effect and size: guess, not run. Marks stack far above the line and overdraw neighbouring messages. Glyph stacks of this kind also raise shaping and layout cost for that message. No crash is known.
- Why it is real: the marks are drawn as received, and clipping on the bubble text path was not found, though it was not proven absent either.
- What tdesktop does: `lib_ui/ui/text/text_block_parser.cpp:21` sets `kMaxDiacAfterSymbol = 2`, and `:596-625` drops bad characters, excess marks, tag characters from U+E0000 up (a crash on some Linux setups), and FE0F on macOS.
- Fix sketch: one pure function at the point text enters the message layout, applying the same rules. It must keep ZWJ and variation selectors that emoji sequences need on the platforms where they are safe.
- Proof plan: unit tests on the filter with Zalgo strings, emoji ZWJ sequences and tag sequences. A demo capture of a fixture message.
- Scope: `src/ui/message_text/`. Low risk apart from the emoji sequences.
