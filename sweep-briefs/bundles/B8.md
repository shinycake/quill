## robustness-1: A large paste freezes the app on every render
- Severity: P0 hang
- Kind: hang
- Platforms: macOS, Windows, Linux (shared Rust on the UI thread)
- Where: `src/ui/composer_ui/send_sticker_pick.rs:681` `text_limit_bar` calls `quill::text_split::units_over_limit` and then `split_markup_text` during render. It is reached from `src/ui/conversation/conversation_part1.rs:341` inside `conversation()`, with no `.cached`. `src/composer/mod.rs:311-312` repeats the split on the UI thread at submit. The cost comes from `src/text_split.rs` `split_markup_text`, which for each part does a full `markup_text_units(&rest)` parse, runs `largest_fitting_prefix` twice, builds a `Vec<(usize, char)>` of all of `rest`, runs `cut_preserves_formatting`, and copies `rest` again. The parser adds its own repeated scans: `src/composer/markup.rs:435` `try_link` and `:409` `try_custom_emoji` run `find("](")` at every `[`, `:247` `parse_top` runs `utf16_to_byte` per merged quote line, `:347` and `:380` scan ahead per marker, and `src/composer/markup_boundary.rs:101` `find_closer` does the same.
- Trigger: paste text over the 4096 limit into the composer. Logs, JSON and quoted text are the bad cases. The brief's million-character paste is the extreme, but 50 KB is enough.
- Effect and size: measured on a standalone `rustc -O` copy of the unmodified parser and splitter, with other builds running on the machine. This is the cost of one split, and it repeats on every render of the conversation while the text stays over the limit.

  | Text | 5 KB | 20 KB | 50 KB | 100 KB | 200 KB |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | Plain words | 0.86 ms | 5.5 ms | 41.5 ms | 98.9 ms | 285 ms |
  | Log lines with `[INFO]` | 2.3 ms | 23 ms | 261 ms | 4.08 s | 13.05 s |
  | JSON | 2.9 ms | 21 ms | 237 ms | 1.81 s | not run |
  | `> ` quote lines | 24.6 ms | 803 ms | 9.54 s | over 60 s (killed) | not run |

  Plain words at 400 KB took 1.12 s, so 1 MB extrapolates to 7 to 10 s (inferred). The user sees the window stop responding after the paste, and each keystroke, or any app notify that re-renders the conversation, buys another stall. Getting out means deleting the text from a frozen field or force-quitting. The text is also kept as a draft, so reopening that chat may wedge again (inferred, not traced to the end).
- Why it is real: the bar only returns early when `over == 0`, so everything over the limit reaches the split. Nothing caches the result per text revision. `095b56414` (2026-10-09) introduced the splitter and its label.
- What tdesktop does: the compose field has no length cap, and `TextUtilities::CutPart` does bounded work per cut, only at send. "Will be sent as N messages" is a Quill-only label. `CharactersLimitLabel` only shows while editing.
- Fix sketch: no split in render. Compute `(units, parts)` once per text revision, off the render path, and let render read that value. Use `units.div_ceil(limit)` as the shown estimate, since the exact part count only matters at send. Make the splitter linear: one parse, a bounded window around each cut, no re-copy of `rest`. Make the parser linear: memoize the next `](`, `)` and newline positions, and track byte offsets instead of calling `utf16_to_byte`. One parse is still 48 ms at 100 KB of log lines, so the per-revision value is required even after the split leaves render.
- Proof plan: a deterministic test with a byte-scan or parse-call counter, asserting linear growth from 10 KB to 1 MB with no wall clock. Then a demo frame-time capture with a 100 KB log fixture in the composer, before and after.
- Scope: `src/text_split.rs`, `src/composer/markup.rs`, `src/composer/markup_boundary.rs`, `src/ui/composer_ui/send_sticker_pick.rs`, `src/composer/mod.rs`. Medium risk, because the split decides real message boundaries and needs parity tests kept. Overlaps the UI-thread lane.
